‹ The Birth of Rama ⋮
Verse 1.1
There was a great and glorious king named Dasharatha who ruled the city of Ayodhya, beloved by his people and renowned throughout the three worlds.
🪷 तपःस्वाध्यायनिरतं — Devoted to austerity and self-study, the best among those learned in speech.
Verse 1.2
He was adorned with all the virtues of a righteous king — learned, wise, and devoted to dharma — a monarch whose fame had spread across all the known realms.
🪷 Firm in his duty, radiant with the glory of truth, his presence was like the sun illuminating the whole world.
Verse 1.3
His four sons — Rama, Bharata, Lakshmana and Shatrughna — were each endowed with extraordinary qualities, strength, and divine grace.
🪷 Like the four Vedas embodied, the pride of the Ikshvaku dynasty, protectors of all creation.
Verse 1.4
Among them, Rama shone like the full moon among stars — of incomparable beauty, serene strength, and boundless compassion for all beings.
🪷 His eyes were like blooming lotus petals, his countenance radiant as a thousand suns, yet cooling as the moon.

What is the deeper meaning of Rama being compared to the full moon?
✨ Valmiki Guide
The moon symbolism for Rama carries profound meaning:

🌕 Coolness — Rama soothes suffering as moonlight relieves heat.
🌕 Fullness — Among all the stars (brothers, peers), Rama alone is complete.
🌕 Constancy — The moon follows its dharmic path, as Rama never deviates from righteousness.
